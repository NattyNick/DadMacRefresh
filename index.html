Part 3 of 8

Back up the whole Mac with Time Machine

Drive: the 2 TB one

Time Machine copies everything: Dad's account, his files, his programs, his Chrome bookmarks and passwords. In Part 7 the new drive gets all of it back from this backup. First the 2 TB drive has to be erased into the Mac's own format, because right now it's in a Windows-friendly format Time Machine won't accept.

Format warning

Anything currently on the 2 TB drive will be gone. If there's anything on it you want, stop and move it somewhere first.

  1. Plug in the 2 TB drive, then open Disk Utility

    Click the magnifying glass 🔍 at the top-right of the screen, type Disk Utility, and press Return.

    Windows translation

    That magnifying glass is Spotlight, the Mac's Start-menu search. Disk Utility is Disk Management.

    If a Time Machine box already popped up asking to use this drive

    Click Don't Use or Decide Later for now. It'll come back later when the drive is in the right format, and that's when we say yes.

  2. Show the whole drive, not just its partition

    From the menu bar: View › Show All Devices. The left list changes: each drive now shows a top line with the manufacturer's name (something like Seagate Expansion Media or WD My Passport) with the drive's current name indented underneath it.

    Click the top line, the manufacturer one, not the indented one. This matters: erasing the top line is what lets you set the partition scheme.

  3. Click Erase in the toolbar and fill in three fields

    1. Name: type Time Machine
    2. Format: choose Mac OS Extended (Journaled) from the list. Not "Encrypted", not "Case-sensitive", not ExFAT.
    3. Scheme: choose GUID Partition Map

    Click Erase. It takes under a minute. Click Done, then quit Disk Utility: Disk Utility › Quit Disk Utility in the menu bar (or press ⌘ Command + Q).

  4. Tell Time Machine to use it

    Right after quitting, a box may pop up: "Do you want to use Time Machine to back up with Time Machine?" If it does: leave Encrypt Backup Disk unchecked and click Use as Backup Disk. Skip to step 5.

    If no box appears: Apple menu → System Preferences… → Time Machine. Click Select Backup Disk…, click Time Machine in the list, leave Encrypt unchecked, click Use Disk.

  5. Start it, then walk away

    The first backup usually starts by itself within two minutes. If you'd rather kick it off: click the small clock-with-arrow icon in the menu bar near the top-right and choose Back Up Now.

    This copies about 110 GB and takes 2 to 3 hours. The Mac may say "Preparing backup…" for a while first; that's normal. Leave the lid open, charger in, drive plugged in. Overnight is fine. Don't let anyone use the computer until it's done.

    How to tell it's finished

    Click the Time Machine menu bar icon. When it says "Latest backup: Today at …" and there's no progress bar, it's done. The Time Machine preferences pane shows the same line.

  6. Look inside the backup, once

    Open a Finder window, click Time Machine under Devices, then double-click your way through: Backups.backupdb → the MacBook's name → the folder with today's date → Macintosh HD → Users → turkeytracks → Pictures. You should see Photos Library sitting there. That's proof the backup holds it.

  7. Now turn automatic backups OFF and eject the drive

    This is the one step that's easy to skip and shouldn't be. Apple menu → System Preferences… → Time Machine → uncheck Back Up Automatically. Then in the Finder sidebar click ⏏ next to Time Machine, wait for it to vanish, and unplug the drive. Put it with the SSD box. It comes back out in Part 7.

    Why this matters

    In Part 4 you'll delete the Photos Library from the Mac. If the drive stayed plugged in with automatic backups on, Time Machine would make a newer backup without the photos, and Part 7 restores from the newest backup. Unplugging freezes the good one.

Checkpoint 3

  • You saw Photos Library inside the backup in Finder.
  • Back Up Automatically is now unchecked.
  • The 2 TB drive is ejected and unplugged.

Part 5 of 8

Upgrade to macOS Monterey

Charger: must stay plugged in the entire time

This is done from a special startup mode called Recovery that downloads Monterey straight from Apple over Wi-Fi. Everything on the Mac stays put; this is an upgrade in place, not a wipe. It also quietly updates the Mac's internal firmware, which is what lets it boot from the new drive later. That's why this comes before the drive swap.

  1. Find the three keys before you need them

    Bottom-left of the keyboard: ⌥ Option (also labeled alt) and next to it ⌘ Command. Top row of letters: R. You'll press all three together and hold them.

  2. Restart and hold the keys right away

    1. Apple menu → Restart… → Restart.
    2. The screen goes black. The moment it does, press and hold ⌥ Option + ⌘ Command + R.
    3. Keep holding until you see a spinning globe with the words "Starting Internet Recovery. This may take a while." Then let go.
    Missed it? No harm done.

    If the normal login screen comes up instead of the globe, just log in, restart again, and hold the keys sooner. You can hold them down before you even click Restart.

  3. Pick your Wi-Fi and wait

    A Wi-Fi menu appears: choose your network, type the password, press Return. A progress bar under the globe fills over 10–30 minutes as it downloads. The screen may go blank and come back. Don't touch anything.

  4. In the "macOS Utilities" window, choose Reinstall macOS

    If the middle option says anything other than "Monterey"

    For example "Reinstall OS X El Capitan" or "Reinstall macOS Sierra". Don't click it. It means the Mac booted the wrong recovery mode, and I'll walk you through the fix.

  5. Click through the installer

    1. Continue on the Monterey welcome screen.
    2. Agree to the license, then Agree again in the little pop-up.
    3. You'll be shown a disk to install to. It should be Macintosh HD and it should be the only one. Click it, then Install (or Unlock and type Dad's password first, if asked).

    Now the long wait: 1 to 2 hours. A progress bar on a black screen, an Apple logo, the screen going dark, the Mac restarting itself two or three times. All normal. The estimate it shows is usually wrong in both directions. Don't close the lid and don't press the power button.

  6. Log in and get through the welcome screens

    Eventually the normal login screen appears with Dad's name. Type his usual password. Monterey then asks a few setup questions:

    • Sign in with your Apple ID: it may already be filled in as turkeytracks@comcast.com. Type the Apple password if it asks. If it offers Set Up Later, that's fine too.
    • Analytics, Screen Time, Siri: click Continue / Set Up Later / Not Now. None of these matter.
    • Choose Your Look: pick Light.

    The desktop looks almost the same as before, just a little rounder. Open Chrome from the Dock and load a website to prove it works.

  7. Confirm the version

    Apple menu → About This Mac. The big words should now be macOS Monterey, version 12.7-something.

Checkpoint 5

Part 6 of 8

Swap in the new drive

Tools: the ★1.2 (P5) and T5 drivers from your Jakemy set

This is one of the easiest repairs on any laptop: ten screws on the bottom, one plug to unhook, one screw holding the drive.

Ground rules

  • Shut down first, then unplug the charger, then wait five minutes so it's cool.
  • Touch the bare metal of the case with your fingers before touching anything inside. Do it again if you get up and come back.
  • Battery plug comes out before the drive, and goes back in after. Always.
  • Screws are tiny. Label the two cups: SHORT and LONG. Work over a towel so a dropped one can't roll away.
  • If anything feels like it needs force, stop. Nothing in here needs force.

  1. Shut down and flip it over

    Apple menu → Shut Down… → Shut Down. Wait for the screen to go completely black and the fan to go silent. Unplug the charger. Close the lid, wait five minutes, then lay it upside down on a towel with the hinge away from you.

  2. Remove the ten bottom screws (the ★1.2 driver)

    1. The eight short screws go in the SHORT cup. The two long ones from the top middle, by the hinge, go in the LONG cup.
    2. Turn each screw counter-clockwise. They're snug but not tight.
    3. Lift the bottom panel off starting at the hinge side and set it aside.
    Why that driver

    The screw heads have five points like a little star, which is what the ★1.2 tip matches. A regular Phillips will not fit and will chew them up.

  3. Unplug the battery

    Touch the metal case first. Then find the battery connector: it's the wide black plug near the edge of the big black battery, with a clear plastic pull-tab on it (photo). Grab the tab and pull it sideways, toward the front edge of the laptop, keeping it flat. It slides out about a quarter inch. Do not lift it upward.

    Why

    Same idea as unplugging a PC tower before opening it. With the battery unhooked, nothing inside has power.

  4. Take out the old drive (the T5 driver)

    The SSD is the narrow stick with the chips on it, near the fan, about the size of a stick of gum. One small screw holds down its free end.

    1. Remove the one screw.
    2. Lift the end you just freed a little, about the thickness of your finger, and pull the stick straight out. It slides about an inch and comes free.
    3. Put the old drive in the new one's bag or box and write OLD 128 GB, has everything on it. It's a spare copy of Dad's whole computer. Keep it.

  5. Put in the new drive

    1. Hold the new SSD by its edges. The connector end has a row of gold contacts with a small gap in it that only lines up one way.
    2. Slide it into the socket at the same slight angle the old one came out at, until the contacts disappear inside. Gentle, even push.
    3. Press the free end down flat and put the T5 screw back in. Just snug.
    If it won't go in

    It's probably upside down or not at the right angle. Take it out, look at the notch in the contacts, and try again. Never push harder.

  6. Plug the battery back in

    Line the connector up with its socket and push it straight in, flat, the reverse of how it came out. It seats with a slight click. Give the pull-tab a tiny tug to confirm it's in.

  7. Bottom panel and ten screws

    Set the panel on, hinge side first, and press around the edges until it clicks flush all the way around. Put the two long screws back in the top-center holes by the hinge, then the other eight. Snug, not tight; if you feel it stop, stop.

Checkpoint 6

  • All ten bottom screws are back in, long ones at the hinge center, and both cups are empty.
  • The old drive is bagged and labeled.
  • The charger is plugged back in and the light on it is on.

All three checked → go to Part 7. The Mac will start up to a blank drive and act confused; that's expected.

If something's off

Things that look scary but aren't

The screen is black and nothing's happening

During Parts 5 and 7 the screen goes fully black for stretches of up to 15 minutes while the Mac restarts itself. Look for the tiny glow of the Apple logo on the lid or listen for the fan. Wait a full 20 minutes before assuming anything. If it's truly off, press the power key once.

A spinning beach ball, or "Preparing…" for ages

Normal in the first minutes of a backup, copy or install. Give it 15 minutes before you worry.

Folder with a flashing question mark

The Mac can't find a system to start from. In Part 7 this is expected on a blank drive. Hold the power key 10 seconds to turn off, then start again holding ⌥ Option + ⌘ Command + R.

"This copy of the Install macOS application is damaged" or "can't be verified"

Almost always the Mac's clock is wrong after sitting powered off. It's a one-line fix from a menu in Recovery; I'll read it to you.

It asks for an Apple ID password I don't have / a verification code

Codes go to Dad's iPhone. Have him read it off. If a screen offers Set Up Later or Skip, that's always fine; I handle the Apple ID in Part 8.

I clicked Not Now on the Migration Assistant screen by mistake

Don't create any account. There's a clean way back, but only if a second account hasn't been made yet, so pause and let me walk you through it.

Something else

Show me the screen. Seeing the exact message beats any description. Then we wait; almost nothing here gets worse by leaving it alone for ten minutes.
